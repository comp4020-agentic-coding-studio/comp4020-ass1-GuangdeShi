First, what does an hour of you cost?

A price tag is written in dollars because dollars are easy to print. You did not get those dollars for nothing — you got them for hours. So before anything can have a price in time, your time needs a price in money.

    What this model is, and is not

    This is an explanatory model, not an official economic or accounting measure. It divides pay by committed hours. It does not account for tax, superannuation, benefits, overtime, unpaid work beyond the commute, or the fact that some hours cost more than others.

    A month is treated as four weeks and a year as fifty-two, exactly as the model was specified. Those two figures do not agree — four weeks, twelve times, is forty-eight — so the same salary entered monthly and yearly gives slightly different rates. The page shows its arithmetic rather than hiding the disagreement.
